A Benchmark for Detecting and Exploiting Weak Structure
            in Stochastic Particle Systems



1     Research vision
This project should be a benchmark and dataset paper, not an architecture paper. The benchmark studies a small team
of mobile collectors operating in a two-dimensional environment filled with many non-learning particles. Individual
particle motion is noisy, but the population may contain weak and persistent structure: a drift, transport corridor,
vortex, or correlated collective pattern. Each collector sees only a local neighbourhood. The scientific problem is to
determine when a team can distinguish genuine structure from random fluctuation and exploit it before the opportunity
disappears.
The key mechanic is irreversible capture growth. When a free particle touches a collector, it attaches permanently.
Later particles can attach to the collector or to its existing aggregate, so an early interception may produce a capture
cascade. This creates a useful ambiguity. A large cascade may indicate that the collectors correctly located a transport
path, or it may be the amplified consequence of one lucky collision. The benchmark must therefore separate three
stages: evidence gathering before contact, the first successful interception, and the growth that follows.
The central question is:

      At what signal strength can locally informed collectors reliably detect and exploit hidden structure in
      stochastic particle motion, and how do coordination and irreversible aggregation change that threshold?

The particles are not learning adversaries. They follow fixed stochastic rules. This makes the first paper interpretable
and reproducible: performance changes can be attributed to observation quality, coordination, signal strength, or
capture geometry rather than to instability from two learning populations. Strategic evasion is a natural later extension,
but it is not part of the initial benchmark.
The contribution should be a controlled environment family, a trajectory dataset, a set of transparent baselines, and
empirical measurements of where reliable exploitation begins. The paper should not claim a new general-purpose
MARL solution. Standard learning methods are included only as benchmark participants.


2     The benchmark
The canonical environment contains M mobile collectors and N particles in a bounded continuous arena. A practical
starting point is M = 4 and N ∈ [250, 500]. Collectors have bounded speed and local sensing. Particles move under a
mixture of stochastic diffusion, local collective interactions, and an optional hidden transport field.

The first release should contain only three environment families:
 • Null motion: diffusion and optional collective interactions, but no planted transport field.
 • Uniform drift: a simple field with controllable direction and strength.

These are enough to test whether a method detects a signal, generalizes across field orientations, and handles structure
more complex than a single direction. Moving fields, reactive avoidance, and multiple interacting swarms should be
postponed.
Each collector observes nearby particle positions and short velocity histories. It does not observe the global field, the
full particle configuration, or the true signal strength. The benchmark should support two information conditions:
independent collectors using only local observations, and collectors allowed to share bounded messages or local
summaries. This isolates the value of coordination without requiring a new coordination architecture.
Two capture conditions should be implemented. In the fixed-geometry condition, a collector keeps a constant capture
radius. In the growing-geometry condition, captured particles remain attached and extend the absorbing boundary.
The comparison reveals whether irreversible aggregation changes only total capture volume or creates a genuinely
path-dependent problem in which early positioning determines later success.
The reward can remain simple: total captured fraction minus small movement and communication costs. The
benchmark should not directly reward classification of the latent field. Signal detection should be evaluated through
behaviour and diagnostics, not supplied as a supervised objective.
The primary difficulty axis is signal-to-noise ratio. For each method, the benchmark estimates the weakest signal at
which performance becomes reliably distinguishable from matched no-signal behaviour. Signal-present and signal-absent
episodes should share initial particle positions and random noise whenever possible, differing only in whether the latent
field is active. This matched design reduces variance and makes causal interpretation much stronger.


3    Dataset, baselines, and empirical claims
Every episode should be saved as a trajectory rather than only as a final score. The released dataset should contain
particle positions and velocities, collector observations and actions, capture events, aggregate geometry, latent-field
parameters, signal labels, environment configuration, and random seed. This supports future work on representation
learning, system identification, offline MARL, imitation, and event prediction without requiring every user to rerun
the simulator.
The benchmark should provide fixed train, validation, and test splits. Test episodes should hold out field orientations,
initial configurations, and random seeds. One stronger out-of-distribution split should hold out either an arena scale,
sensing radius, or signal-strength interval. The release should also include a compact subset that can be evaluated on
a workstation and a full dataset generated on the HPC system.
The baseline suite should be small and interpretable:
 • random movement;
 • systematic coverage;
 • local-density chasing;
 • a scripted local flow estimator that follows estimated particle velocity;
 • independent recurrent PPO;
 • one standard cooperative MARL method, such as MAPPO;
 • a privileged oracle that observes the true field.
The scripted flow-estimation baseline is essential. It shows whether the signal is usable with local temporal information
even if learned agents fail. The privileged oracle shows the maximum value available from knowing the latent field.
Together they prevent a weak MARL result from being mistaken for proof that the task itself is impossible.
The paper should target four empirical claims.
Claim 1: A detectability boundary exists. As signal strength increases, methods move from chance-level
behaviour to reliable interception. The paper should estimate this transition rather than report performance at one
arbitrary noise setting.
Claim 2: Aggregation amplifies both signal and accident. Growing geometry should improve post-contact
capture when a true transport structure exists, but may also increase false cascades under null motion. Reporting
both effects is more informative than reporting total capture alone.
Claim 3: Coordination is most useful near the boundary. At very low signal, no method has enough information.
At very high signal, individual collectors may already succeed. The largest coordination advantage should appear in

the intermediate regime where distributed observations jointly resolve an otherwise ambiguous pattern.
Claim 4: Difficulty shifts under scale. A restrained scaling study should vary collector count and one environmental
axis, preferably arena size or sensing radius. The result should show how the detectability boundary moves when the
same local sensing capability is applied to a larger or more weakly observed system.
The three indispensable figures are therefore:

1. success or capture versus signal strength for all baselines;
2. fixed versus growing geometry, including false cascades under null motion;
3. independent versus coordinated collectors around the transition region.

A fourth scaling figure is valuable but not required for the minimum viable paper.


4    Two-week empirical plan
The HPC deadline means the project must be frozen quickly. The goal is not to exhaust every design possibility. It is
to leave the two-week window with a complete canonical dataset and the runs needed for the paper’s central figures.
Days 1–2: Freeze the simulator. Implement null motion, uniform drift, one curved field, local observations, fixed
and growing capture, deterministic seeds, and matched signal/no-signal episodes. Select one canonical arena, collector
count, particle count, and episode horizon. After Day 2, modify mechanics only to fix correctness bugs.
Days 3–4: Validate with transparent baselines. Run random, coverage, density, flow-estimation, and oracle
policies using a few seeds. Identify a signal range containing clear failure, a transition region, and clear success.
Confirm that growing geometry changes outcomes in a path-dependent way rather than simply enlarging every score.
Days 5–8: Run the main detectability sweep. Use roughly six to eight signal levels. Start with five seeds per
cell for all baselines. Allocate additional seeds only near the transition and for headline comparisons. Run fixed and
growing geometry with matched null controls.
Days 9–10: Run the coordination comparison. Compare independent recurrent PPO and one standard
cooperative MARL baseline at one low, two intermediate, and one high signal level. Avoid broad hyperparameter
sweeps. The purpose is to locate where information sharing matters, not to crown an architecture.
Days 11–12: Run one scaling study. Vary collector count and either arena size or sensing radius. Use fewer signal
points concentrated near the canonical transition. Do not add another environment family at this stage.
Days 13–14: Verify and freeze. Rerun missing or anomalous cells, calculate seed-level bootstrap intervals, generate
final figures, export the complete trajectory dataset, and freeze all configurations and code. Exploratory jobs should
stop once the required figure cells are complete.
The minimum successful outcome after two weeks is not a finished polished paper. It is a frozen benchmark, a
documented dataset, and enough validated runs to support the first three claims. Writing, analysis refinement, and
additional lightweight baselines can continue after HPC access ends.
The paper’s concise contribution statement should be:

     We introduce a controlled benchmark and trajectory dataset for measuring when multi-agent collectors
     can detect and exploit weak structure in stochastic particle motion. The benchmark separates pre-contact
     detection from post-contact aggregation, quantifies noise-induced capture cascades through matched null
     episodes, and measures how coordination and environmental scale shift the signal strength required for
     reliable collection.
