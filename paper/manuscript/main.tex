\title{Detecting Weak Structure in Stochastic Particle Systems:\\
A Matched-Counterfactual Multi-Collector Benchmark}
\author{Anonymous Authors}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We specify a benchmark for measuring when a team of locally observing mobile
collectors can behaviorally exploit a weak latent transport field in the
motion of many stochastic, non-learning particles.  The primary design couples
each signal episode to a no-signal episode with identical initial states,
Brownian forcing, field geometry, and random-number provenance.  It separates
pre-contact search and first interception from irreversible post-contact
aggregation.  The proposed primary outcome is the matched reduction in
time-to-first interception under a fixed local-flow policy, reported against a
dimensionless per-step drift-to-Brownian ratio and with a grid-censored
boundary.  Transparent random, passive, coverage, flow-following, and
privileged controls precede recurrent IPPO and MAPPO.  This document currently
records the formal design and correctness requirements.  \textbf{No benchmark
result or detectability-boundary claim is reported.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Many local observations of stochastic motion may contain a weak common
structure even when any individual displacement is dominated by noise.  A
mobile team can, in principle, use those observations to intercept particle
flow.  In practice, a large collection event can also result from passive flux
or one lucky contact amplified by growing capture geometry.

We therefore ask one question: \emph{what is the weakest latent-field signal
strength at which a team of locally observing collectors achieves a reliably
positive matched improvement in pre-contact first-interception performance
over otherwise identical no-signal episodes?}

The closest current work already studies a locally informed mobile collector
capturing passive particles in chaotic flow~\cite{wang2025collector}, and
reinforcement learning has already produced collective foraging in locally
observing active particles~\cite{loeffler2023foraging}.  We do not claim the
first mobile particle collector, vortex-flow collection problem, particle
foraging system, or local-sensing learning formulation.  The proposed
contribution is a controlled multi-collector measurement instrument combining
matched null episodes, a policy-relative detectability boundary, explicit
first-contact/cascade separation, and a closed trajectory/provenance schema.
No trajectory dataset has yet been generated or released.

\section{Canonical stochastic system}

The canonical domain is the unit square with reflecting boundaries.  Four
collectors act in a population of \(N=256\) non-learning particles.  Before
capture, particle \(k\) follows
\[
 x_{k,t+1} =
 \mathcal{R}\!\left(
 x_{k,t} + \Delta t\,\alpha b_z(x_{k,t})
 + \sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}\,\epsilon_{k,t}
 \right),
\]
where \(\epsilon_{k,t}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_2)\), \(\mathcal{R}\) is exact
reflection, \(\alpha\) is field strength, and \(b_z\) is null, uniform, or
vortex flow.  For normalized collector action \(a_{i,t}\in\mathbb{R}^2\), the
implemented collector transition is
\[
 y_{i,t+1}=\mathcal{R}\!\left(y_{i,t}+\Delta t\,v_{\max}
 \frac{a_{i,t}}{\max\{1,\lVert a_{i,t}\rVert_2\}}\right).
\]
Thus every action is projected onto the unit Euclidean ball before a common
speed limit is applied.  The current canonical values are
\(M=4\), \(N=256\), \(H=400\), \(\Delta t=0.02\),
\(\sigma=0.06\), and \(v_{\max}=0.12\) in the unit square.

At reset, collectors occupy the deterministic ID-ordered lattice
\((.25,.25),(.25,.75),(.75,.25),(.75,.75)\).  Particle positions are sampled
from an initialization-specific random stream by bounded rejection sampling
until every particle is strictly outside all initial capture discs, including
a numerical clearance.  Reset also pre-generates the complete Brownian tensor
for the episode and draws a uniform-field orientation once from a separate
field stream.  Initialization, Brownian forcing, field nuisance variables,
tie breaking, and random-policy actions have distinct named streams.

The primary condition uses a uniform field and fixed point-particle capture
geometry.  A deterministic \texttt{local\_flow\_v1} research policy remains to
be frozen; the current stationary, random, and privileged field-aware policies
are execution diagnostics rather than scientific baselines.

\section{Causal local information contract}

Collector \(i\)'s implemented observation contains its arena-normalized
position; up to \(K=32\) nearest free particles within sensing radius
\(r_s=0.16\), ordered by distance and particle ID; and the other collectors'
relative positions, normalized by arena dimensions and ordered by collector
ID.  Each populated particle row contains relative position divided by
\(r_s\), apparent endpoint finite-difference particle velocity, and normalized radial
distance, together with presence and velocity-validity masks.  Velocity is
revealed only when that particle was visible to the same collector in both
consecutive observations.  A newly visible particle therefore receives a zero
velocity entry and a false validity mask.

The observation constructor accepts neither field strength or orientation,
global state, a random generator, nor future noise.  Tests additionally check
that changing an out-of-radius particle, changing future Brownian draws, or
toggling signal strength before motion cannot change the reset observation.
These checks validate the implemented interface; they do not prove that a
future policy or adapter cannot introduce leakage.

\section{Environment transition order}

For each one-based environment step, the implementation applies the following
order:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item project and advance collector actions, with exact reflection;
  \item advance all free particles with that step's pre-generated Brownian
        slice and the episode field, again with exact reflection;
  \item compute finite-difference particle velocities;
  \item resolve captures, assign each newly captured particle one permanent
        owner, and award its collector one unit of reward;
  \item increment the step counter, record the first nonempty capture step,
        update consecutive-visibility masks, and evaluate all-captured
        termination or horizon truncation;
  \item construct the next local observations.
\end{enumerate}
The current tie resolver uses a dedicated but stateful random stream.  It is
not yet suitable for the primary comparison after signal/null trajectories
diverge; event-keyed tie breaking is a blocking requirement.

\section{Matched primary estimand}

For scenario seed \(s\), signal strength \(\alpha\), horizon \(H\), and
condition \(c\in\{0,\alpha\}\), define
\[
T^*_{s,c} =
\begin{cases}
\text{first capture step in }\{1,\ldots,H\}, & \text{capture occurs},\\
H+1, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\]
The paired improvement is
\[
D_s(\alpha)=\frac{T^*_{s,0}-T^*_{s,\alpha}}{H},
\qquad
\delta(\alpha)=\mathbb{E}_s[D_s(\alpha)].
\]
Positive values mean that the signal episode intercepted earlier than its
matched null episode.  The dimensionless primary x-axis is the per-step
drift-to-Brownian ratio
\[
\rho=\frac{\alpha\sqrt{\Delta t}}{\sigma}.
\]

Complete scenario-seed blocks, not particles, collectors, or time steps, are
the sampling units.  The primary boundary is the smallest tested grid point
whose one-sided simultaneous 95\% lower confidence bound exceeds zero.  It is
reported as a grid interval or as censored; continuous logistic or isotonic
crossings are sensitivity analyses only.

\section{Correctness gates}

The simulator must establish deterministic random streams, exact zero-signal
equivalence, Brownian scaling, uniform-field displacement, multi-wall
reflection, single permanent capture ownership, reproducible ties, absence of
field leakage, and matched signal/null provenance.  Fixed and growing
geometries must agree through first contact under matched actions and noise.
Failure blocks performance experiments.

The present correctness suite contains 44 passing tests under Python 3.12.13
and NumPy 2.3.5.  It covers deterministic capture-free resets over 100 canonical
seeds, action and speed limits, exact reflected motion, causal visibility
masks, forbidden-information checks, fixed-ID teammate ordering, one-draw
field orientation, paired random actions, complete zero-signal rollout
identity, the zero-diffusion stationary limit, capture ownership, one-based
first contact, horizon truncation, schema parsing, and a deterministic
high-signal oracle calibration microcase.  This is engineering evidence only:
none of the tests estimates \(\delta(\alpha)\), compares baseline performance,
or establishes a multi-agent mechanism.

\section{Evaluation plan}

Scripted policies are evaluated before learning: stationary/passive, random,
coverage, density-greedy, local-flow, team-flow, and privileged field-aware
controls.  Single-collector and independent multi-collector controls separate
parallel swept area from coordination.  Recurrent IPPO and standard MAPPO are
benchmark participants only after scripted and data-validation gates pass.

Growing capture, bounded shared summaries, vortex fields, and collector-count
scaling are secondary analyses.  An aggregation claim additionally requires an
area- or perimeter-matched non-growing control.  A coordination claim requires
matched information bandwidth, policy capacity, action budget, and evaluation
conditions.

\section{Trajectory and provenance contracts}

Two closed JSON Schema documents specify, but do not yet produce, the planned
artifacts.  A trajectory-step record requires a schema version, run and
scenario identifiers, condition and policy identifiers, the step index,
particle and collector positions, collector actions, capture ownership and
events, and termination flags.  A run manifest requires the repository branch
and 40-character commit, dirty-worktree flag, environment-config path and
SHA-256 digest, paired-condition declarations, policy configuration and
privilege flag, Python/NumPy/platform/command provenance, and artifact byte
counts and SHA-256 digests.  Both schemas reject unspecified top-level fields.
A writer, schema-instance validation, checksumming pipeline, and generated
dataset are still absent.

\section{Current evidence status and blockers}

The deterministic core can reset and execute complete bounded episodes, build
causal local observations, run three smoke policies, and represent planned
trajectory and manifest records.  \textbf{There is no scientific performance
result, generated dataset, detectability-boundary estimate, aggregation
evidence, or coordination evidence.}  All active claims therefore remain
proposed.

\pending{Before any performance pilot: replace stateful ties with event-keyed
ties; implement and validate a complete matched null/signal episode wrapper;
resolve endpoint-only capture with continuous-contact handling or a frozen
timestep-convergence criterion;
freeze \texttt{local\_flow\_v1} using only causal masked observations; write,
validate, and hash trajectories and manifests; and freeze the growing attached
disc's radius, motion, wall, and visualization semantics.  A PettingZoo/Gym
adapter, statistical boundary estimator, figures, tables, and learned-policy
runs remain later work.}

\section{Limitations}

The boundary is policy-, horizon-, field-, geometry-, and resource-relative,
not a universal physical critical point.  Common random numbers reduce
variance only when their coupling remains causally coherent.  A positive
paired effect can still arise from passive transport, so random, stationary,
and coverage controls govern the interpretation.  Without an isolated effect
of complementary local information or coordination, the benchmark's AAMAS fit
would remain weak.
