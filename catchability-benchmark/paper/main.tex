\begin{abstract}
Multi-agent control in stochastic physical systems is often evaluated at one
arbitrary choice of length and time units, obscuring whether a coordination
effect reflects a dimensionless regime or a scale-specific policy
representation. We study four collectors acting on diffusing particles under a
latent drift field. A matched-seed factorial varies drift signal-to-noise
$\rhoSNR$, drift-to-control ratio $\kappaC$, and absolute transport scale
$\etaT$, and compares a bounded shared velocity summary against a
capacity-matched independent controller. We evaluate whether $(\rhoSNR,
\kappaC)$ predict held-out coordination gain without $\etaT$ using a
pre-registered leave-one-cell-out criterion. We then train independent and
communicating policies at one physical scale and test zero-shot transfer to
dynamically equivalent rescalings under raw versus dimensionless observations.
The implementation passes an exact rescaling audit after a preserved,
versioned correction for physical-unit clipping. \pending{Insert the registered

\section{Introduction}

Changing metres to centimetres should not change a physical law, but it can
change the numerical inputs received by a learned controller. Dimensionless
quantities provide invariants of such unit transformations
\citep{villar2023dimensionless}; reinforcement-learning generalization asks
whether policies retain behavior outside their training domain
\citep{sonar2021invariant}. The interaction becomes especially consequential in
multi-agent systems: communication can aggregate information, yet a learned
message may encode the accidental scale of the training environment.

We use stochastic particle collection as a controlled setting in which both the
physical scaling and the coordination intervention are explicit. Four mobile
collectors observe only nearby particles. The treatment replaces four local
velocity summaries with a bounded team summary while preserving action and
message capacity. The central scientific question is not merely whether this
summary helps at one operating point, but which dimensionless groups determine
its causal gain.

Our candidate contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item a matched-seed $3\times3\times3$ benchmark for the coordination-gain
  surface over three derived catchability groups;
  \item a held-out predictive test of whether absolute transport scale $\etaT$
  is required beyond $(\rhoSNR,\kappaC)$;
  \item a controlled representation intervention comparing zero-shot transfer
  of independent and communicating policies under raw and dimensionless inputs;
  \item an auditable execution package with immutable manifests, exact seed
  firewalls, and failed-first rescaling provenance.
\end{enumerate}

Items 2 and 3 are conditional on the registered results. A non-rejection of the
two-axis null is not treated as proof of sufficiency.

\section{Related Work}

\noindent\textbf{Dimensionless learning.}
The Buckingham $\Pi$ theorem characterizes physical relationships through
dimensionless groups \citep{buckingham1914}. Recent work imposes units
equivariance by constructing dimensionless features before learning
\citep{villar2023dimensionless}, or discovers useful groups from data under
dimensional constraints \citep{bakarji2022buckingham}. We derive candidate
groups from known simulator dimensions and test their predictive sufficiency
for a multi-agent policy contrast.

\paragraph{Policy transfer.}
Invariant Policy Optimization seeks representations supporting policies that
generalize across training domains \citep{sonar2021invariant}. Domain
randomization instead trains across ensembles of perturbed simulators
\citep{muratore2018domain}. Our intervention trains at one scale and compares
representations under transformations known to preserve the dimensionless
dynamics; it is not a new optimization algorithm or sim-to-real guarantee.

\paragraph{Cooperative learning and communication.}
IPPO and MAPPO are strong PPO-based cooperative baselines
\citep{yu2022surprising}. CommNet jointly learns continuous, mean-pooled
communication and behavior \citep{sukhbaatar2016commnet}. We use IPPO and
CommNet to separate independent execution from learned communication while
holding the representation intervention fixed.

\section{Dimensionless Catchability}

Let $L$ denote characteristic arena length, $\alpha$ drift speed, $\sigma$ the
diffusion coefficient under the simulator convention, $\Delta t$ the step, and
$v_{\max}$ collector speed. The frozen groups are
\begin{equation}
  \rhoSNR=\frac{\alpha\sqrt{\Delta t}}{\sigma},\qquad
  \kappaC=\frac{\alpha}{v_{\max}},\qquad
  \etaT=\frac{\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t}}{L}.
\end{equation}
They imply normalized one-step drift and control displacements
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\alpha\Delta t}{L}=\rhoSNR\etaT,
  \qquad
  \frac{v_{\max}\Delta t}{L}=\frac{\rhoSNR\etaT}{\kappaC}.
\end{equation}
Thus $(\rhoSNR,\kappaC)$ alone do not determine normalized transport. Whether
$\etaT$ materially improves prediction of coordination gain is an empirical,
held-out question.

\section{Registered Scripted Benchmark}

\paragraph{Environment and contrast.}
The unit-square benchmark contains 256 particles, four collectors, a 67-step
horizon, sensing radius $0.16$, and fixed swept-contact geometry. Common random
numbers pair every policy within every cell and seed. The primary estimand is
\begin{equation}
 D(\rhoSNR,\kappaC,\etaT)
 =\mathbb{E}[Y_{\mathrm{shared}}-Y_{\mathrm{independent}}],
\end{equation}
where $Y$ is unique team capture yield.

\paragraph{Factorial and seed panel.}
Each group is varied by a factor of two below and above the executed SPS-C03
anchor, producing 27 cells. Sixty-four fresh seeds are shared across cells; two
diagnostic policies give a total of four arms and 6,912 episodes.

\paragraph{Predictive decision rule.}
Quadratic response surfaces using $(\rhoSNR,\kappaC)$ and
$(\rhoSNR,\kappaC,\etaT)$ are compared by leave-one-cell-out RMSE. Two-axis
sufficiency is rejected only if the observed RMSE ratio is at most $0.80$ and
the one-sided 95\% common-seed bootstrap lower bound for
$\mathrm{RMSE}_{2}-\mathrm{RMSE}_{3}$ exceeds zero.

\paragraph{Correctness audit.}
Four physical rescalings preserve all three groups and normalized geometry.
The final audit passed all eight seed-policy comparisons for capture yield and
quantized normalized final state. The initial audit and its physical-unit
clipping failure remain in the repository.

\section{Learned Scale Transfer}

We train IPPO and CommNet at the canonical scale under two observation maps.
Raw inputs retain physical length and velocity units. Dimensionless inputs use
$x/L$, relative positions divided by sensing radius, and $v\Delta t/r_s$.
Both maps expose identical entities, masks, and slot counts, and both use
standardization statistics fitted only on canonical training trajectories.

Frozen checkpoints are evaluated without fine-tuning on the canonical,
length-$\times2$, time-$\times4$, and mixed-half variants. The representation
estimand is a paired difference-in-differences in additive capture yield; ratios
are avoided because collapsed canonical policies make retention ratios
unstable. CommNet is also evaluated with received messages zeroed.

\section{Limitations}

The factorial holds horizon, normalized geometry, density, team size, sensing,
field family, and controller definitions fixed. It cannot establish a universal
three-parameter law. Power calibration targets an $\etaT$ effect spread across
the surface and is weaker for sparse cell-specific interactions. Exact scripted
rescaling does not imply neural policies will transfer. The learned study uses
one stochastic particle substrate and at most two primary architectures; any
sim-to-real interpretation would require domain calibration not attempted here.
